# M46.20 — Made for You

**Asked by the user (2026-11-02):** "theres softwares called QuietPlay and Musike, i want you to just see how they have a Made for You page and do that, also from the forks we already have, personalized stuff".

## What the references do (research, 2026-11-02)
- **QuietPlay** (closed source; its docs):
  - Home has a "Made for you" box with Mixes, New songs and Playlists.
  - It learns from plays, finished plays, skips, likes, songs played one after another and the hour of day. Only top artist names are sent to Deezer or Apple.
  - Mixes: Made for you, Because you played X, On repeat, Never skipped, an afternoon mix by time of day, and genre mixes.
  - Playlists for you: Discovery mix, More from your favorites, "Because you play <artist>", built from up to 24 seed artists and their Deezer related artists.
  - Stat cards: hours this week, top artist this month, most played song, peak hour.
- **Musike** (MIT): no personalization. Home has a greeting, Recently played and Your favorites; the radio is YouTube Music's automix.
- **Forks:**
  - RiPlay: quick picks seeded by most played and last played, forgotten favorites (last played over 90 days ago), new releases.
  - Metrolist: quick picks from the related songs of recent and top plays, forgotten favorites, "Similar to".

## Goal
- **Made for You** (`/music/for-you`), with shelves:
  - Daily Mixes: the user's songs grouped by main genre (by artist while songs aren't tagged), with similar artists' songs mixed in, one in three.
  - Made for you:
    - Discover Weekly: songs the user hasn't played, by artists like theirs; new each week.
    - Release Radar: the last four weeks' releases by their artists.
    - On Repeat: the most played of the last 30 days.
    - A mix for this time of day (morning, afternoon, evening or night), from other days.
    - Repeat Rewind: songs played three times or more but not in the last 30 days.
  - Because you listened to <artist> (the top three artists' neighbours), and radios for the two top artists.
  - Genre and mood mixes: the user's songs of their top genres and moods with the genre sources' songs (M46.19).
  - Your listening: minutes this week, top artist and song this month, peak hour.
- **A mix page** (`/music/mix`): Play, Shuffle, Save as a playlist.
- **Where to find it:**
  - Home's new "Made for you" row (a layout section, first after Continue).
  - Browse all's first tile.
  - A refresh in the top bar.
- **Sources:** a new provider kind `music.recommend` (`similarArtists`, `topTracks`, `newReleases`, `radio`, each optional). Deezer implements all four: related artists, top songs, albums from the last weeks, artist radio.

## Decisions
- **What counts:**
  - Weights are plays with a 30-day half-life, plus 3 for a favorite.
  - Artists are main artists ("A feat. B" → A).
  - A mix needs 6 songs to be shown.
- **When mixes are made:**
  - Once per local day (the UI passes its UTC offset), in the background, kept in `local_value` `music.madeForYou`.
  - Opening the page shows the last ones while new ones are made; `MadeForYouEvent` refreshes the page.
  - Each mix's order is a steady shuffle seeded by the day (Discover Weekly and Release Radar by the week).
- **Asking sources:**
  - Each artist is asked about once per making (similar artists and top songs are memoized), one call at a time, to stay inside Deezer's limit.
  - A failing source only leaves its mixes out.
- **Names:** mix names are made by the UI (`forYou.titles.*`), so they're translated. Rust gives a kind, a seed (artist, genre, mood or time of day), a number and the first three artists.
- **Picking the artist (Deezer 1.3.2):**
  - Deezer's artist search can list a namesake first. "Michael Jackson" gave an account with 232 fans before the one with 13 million, and the namesake has no related artists or radio.
  - So the extension takes the exact-name match with the most fans, and caches only artists it found, so a timeout doesn't stick.
- **A making with nothing in it isn't kept:** when songs are known but every mix came back empty (the sources didn't answer), nothing is stored and the next open tries again.
- **Skips:** signals stop at plays and favorites. Skips and "never skipped" need playback to report how far a song got before the next one; that's left for later (noted in feature parity).

## Verification
- **Tests:**
  - taste: main artists, steady shuffles, On Repeat and Repeat Rewind, Daily Mixes by genre and by artist, mood mixes, top artists, stats, the time-of-day mix;
  - interleaving and local days; `plays_since`;
  - host: recommendations from the first source with an answer (a failing one gives way);
  - UI: shelves and numbers, making and refreshing, a mix playing and saving, Home's row, the empty state.
  - All app tests (424), clippy, `cargo test --workspace`.
- **Live check:** Deezer's recommendations answered: similar to Daft Punk (Justice, Cassius, The Chemical Brothers…), top songs (One More Time), radio, and a year's releases (Taylor Swift).
- **Windows (release build, the test history of a few dozen plays):**
  - About 30 s after start, Home's "Made for You" row showed Discover Weekly, Release Radar, three "Because you listened to" mixes, Ray Parker Jr. Radio, Julian Casablancas Radio and an Energetic Mix.
  - No Daily Mix or On Repeat: too few songs per genre and repeated plays in this history.
  - The page grouped them into shelves. "Your listening" read 38 minutes this week, top artist Bobby "Boris" Pickett, top song Monster Mash, peak 3 PM.
  - Some descriptions showed a raw key (`forYou.about.becauseYouListened`); every kind now has its line.
  - Discover Weekly (30 songs) played from its first song. Save as playlist made a 30-song playlist with albums filled in; it was deleted afterwards.
- **Android (debug build):**
  - The first making gave no mixes: Deezer timed out at start, and its search answered with the namesake Michael Jackson. That led to the two decisions above.
  - With Deezer 1.3.2, Home showed Discover Weekly, "Because you listened to Michael Jackson" and Michael Jackson Radio. They still showed on the final build, which carries 1.3.2 itself (the pushed copy was removed).
