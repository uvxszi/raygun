# M46.12 — Sound and lyrics editing

**Asked by the user (2026-11-01, queued; started 2026-11-02 with "Start sound editing"):**
- edit lyrics (words and timings) and shift them by an offset; the same for video subtitles;
- per-song start and end trims and per-song volume;
- loudness normalization, an equalizer and other sound effects for all songs;
- crossfade into the next song, adjustable or off;
- desktop through Web Audio, Android through Media3's audio processors. YouTube's embedded player can only change its volume.

**Relevant docs:** ARCHITECTURE §6.2 (engines), M09 (Android engine), M15 (lyrics), M45 and M46.4 (video players, subtitle delay), M46.8 (speed, panels), research `docs/research/music.md` (RiPlay's equalizer, normalization, crossfade; Nuclear's crossfade and lyrics offset).

## Goal
- **Sound settings** (Settings → Sound, and an Equalizer button in the full player and the side panel):
  - a 10-band equalizer (31 Hz–16 kHz, ±12 dB) with presets and a preamp;
  - bass boost, reverb (rooms and halls), balance, mono;
  - loudness normalization to a target, learned per song;
  - crossfade between songs, 0–12 s (off by default);
  - a limiter so boosts never clip.
- **Per song** (a song's menu → "Edit sound and lyrics"):
  - start and end trims, set by typing or from the playing position;
  - its own volume, ±12 dB;
  - lyrics: a timing offset, editing lines and their times, tap-along syncing, adding lyrics when no source has them, and going back to the source's.
- **Video subtitles:** the delay both players have, plus editing a cue's words and times in the desktop player; edits are kept per title.
- **Engines:**
  - Desktop: the `<audio>` engine runs through a Web Audio graph (two elements for crossfades). YouTube's embed gets per-song volume and volume fades only.
  - Android: Media3 audio processors in the playback service (two players for crossfades).

## Research (2026-11-02, reference apps)
- **RiPlay:**
  - Its equalizer is the platform `Equalizer` on the session, so the bands are whatever the phone offers (often 5).
  - Its presets are 10-point shapes in 0–1 (dB = (v − 0.5) × 16): Flat, Bass boost, Rock, Jazz, Vocal, Classical, Pop, Electronic, Dance, Acoustic.
  - It subtracts the largest boost from every band (automatic headroom), and a "linked" mode moves all bands together.
  - Bass boost is `BassBoost`, reverb is `PresetReverb` as an aux effect (Small/Medium/Large room, Medium/Large hall, Plate), and normalization is `LoudnessEnhancer` fed by YouTube's `loudnessDb`.
  - Its crossfade fades out, switches, then fades back in, on one player (Off, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 12 s).
  - It has no per-song trims or volume, and its lyrics editing is a raw text box.
- **Nuclear:**
  - Its hifi package (unused by its player) chains BiquadFilters: a low shelf first, a high shelf last, and peaking bands with Q = 2f / (f₊ − f₋) between them.
  - Its lyrics offset is per session and resets on every song: lines show at `position − offset` and seek to `line + offset`.
- **Subtitles:** cloudstream sets the offset by tapping a cue when it's heard (`cue.start − position`). aniyomi has "heard/seen" stopwatch buttons. Neither edits cues.

## Design
- **Settings** (`Settings::sound`, `SoundPrefs`, one row):
  - The equalizer: on/off, a preset id, 10 gains in tenths of a dB (31, 62, 125, 250, 500 Hz, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 kHz; ±12 dB) and a preamp.
  - Effects: bass boost (0–100), reverb (off or one of RiPlay's six), balance (−100…100), mono.
  - Normalization (on/off, target −23…−8 LUFS, default −14) and crossfade (0–12 s).
  - Presets live in the UI (`sound/presets.ts`, RiPlay's shapes on our fixed bands); a preset fills the bands.
  - Engines get the settings through `EngineCommand::SetEffects`, sent with every load and on change.
- **Per song** (`song_edit`, keyed by track key, stamped for sync): start and end trims in ms, gain in tenths of a dB, lyrics offset in ms, and edited lyrics (JSON). Commands `song_edit_get`/`song_edit_set` take a `TrackQuery`; saving an edit with nothing changed forgets it (a tombstone). `lyrics_get` answers with the edits applied and says so (`edited`, `offsetMs`).
- **Loudness** (`local_track_loudness`): the engines measure K-weighted loudness while a song plays (gated like BS.1770) and report it (`EngineEvent::Loudness`). The next play gets `target − lufs` as gain. On the first play the desktop engine levels live from its running measurement. YouTube's embed can't be measured; YouTube normalizes its own videos.
- **Loads:** `EngineCommand::Load` carries `gainDb` (song volume + normalization), `endMs` (end trim), `fadeMs` (crossfade) and `measure` (loudness unknown). Trims change where a song starts when it starts from the top. `SetTrackGain`/`SetEnd` update the playing song when its edit changes.
- **Crossfade:**
  - The core starts the next song when the playing one is within the crossfade of its end (or end trim). It doesn't with repeat one, the sleep timer's "end of song", or nothing next.
  - The engine fades the old song out while the new one fades in (two elements, or two players on Android).
  - A lookup that is still resolving lets the old song play on.
  - Between the `<audio>` engine and YouTube's embed, the old one fades its volume out instead of stopping.
- **Desktop graph** (per `<audio>` element):
  - per track: source → track gain (song volume, normalization, fades) → mix;
  - for measuring: track → K-weighting filters → analyser.
  - The mix runs through the preamp, 10 BiquadFilters (Nuclear's shelf/peaking layout), the bass low shelf, reverb (ConvolverNode with a generated impulse per preset, wet/dry), balance (StereoPanner) and mono (channel count 1), a limiter (DynamicsCompressor at −1 dBFS) and the master volume.
  - `crossOrigin = 'anonymous'`: the stream proxy already answers with CORS, which Web Audio needs to hear a stream.
- **Android:**
  - `SoundProcessor`, a Media3 `AudioProcessor` on 16-bit and float PCM, does the same chain (biquads, bass shelf, balance, mono, gain with fades, limiter) and measures loudness. The fixed 10 bands sound the same on every phone, unlike the platform equalizer.
  - Reverb is a `PresetReverb` aux effect on the player's session, as RiPlay does it.
  - Crossfade uses a second ExoPlayer: the session moves to the new player (`MediaSession.setPlayer`) while the old one fades out and is released.
- **Lyrics editor** (part 2): lines with times, editing words, adding and removing lines, tap-along (the playing position stamps the next line), an offset stepper (±100 ms, kept per song), "Use the source's lyrics".
- **Subtitles** (part 2): the desktop player's cue list (tap a cue when it's heard sets the delay, like cloudstream), and editing a cue's words and times, saved per title and track.

## Done-check
- **Rust:** the core starts the next song early for a crossfade (not with repeat one, the sleep timer's end of song, or at the queue's end); trims change the start and the end; loudness is stored and turns into gain; edits are saved and returned with lyrics.
- **Vitest:** the Web Audio graph (fake context): EQ gains, crossfade ramps, end trims, live levelling; the equalizer screen and presets; the song editor (trims, volume, lyrics lines, offset, tap-along).
- 🖥️ **Windows:** hear the EQ and a crossfade, trim a song, change a song's volume, edit and shift lyrics, edit a subtitle cue.
- 📱 **Android:** the Kotlin compiles; the emulator plays with the EQ and a crossfade.

## Decisions
- **Done in two parts.** Part 1 is the sound: the equalizer and effects, normalization, crossfade, and each song's trims and volume, on both platforms. Part 2 is the lyrics editor and subtitle cue editing.
- **Fixed bands, not the phone's equalizer.** RiPlay uses whatever bands the device offers. Raygun's ten octave bands are its own biquads on both platforms, so a preset sounds the same on desktop and on any phone. The presets are RiPlay's ten shapes on those bands, plus seven of Raygun's own (Hip-hop, Loudness, treble and bass boosts and cuts, Small speakers, Spoken word).
- **Headroom:**
  - The largest boost (band or bass) comes off before the equalizer, as RiPlay does, so boosting never clips.
  - A limiter at −1 dBFS catches what's left (normalization boosts, reverb).
  - The preamp is there to make up the level by ear.
- **Desktop engine:**
  - Two `<audio>` elements take turns; each goes through `createMediaElementSource` into its own level and fade gains, then one shared chain.
  - The master volume moved from the element to a gain node, since what an element's volume does once it feeds Web Audio isn't the same everywhere.
  - Without Web Audio (tests, or if creating the context fails), the elements play as before; a song's gain can then only turn it down.
- **Crossfade:**
  - The core starts the next song when the playing one is within the crossfade of its end, so it works the same for every engine.
  - The fade length is what's left of the song at that moment.
  - Fades are equal-power (sine in, cosine out), so the middle doesn't dip.
  - A song still being looked up lets the old one play on; it only fades in if the old one is still playing when its stream arrives.
  - Skipping by hand cuts, as Spotify does.
  - Into or out of YouTube's embed, the embed's volume steps every 100 ms (its API has nothing smoother).
- **Android crossfade:** a second ExoPlayer with its own processor; the session moves to it with `MediaSession.setPlayer`, and audio focus moves too (only the session's player handles focus, or the new one's request would pause the old). The old player fades out in its processor and is stopped.
- **Loudness:**
  - Measured, not fetched. RiPlay reads YouTube's `loudnessDb`, which Raygun never sees (it plays YouTube through the embed).
  - Both engines measure the song itself with BS.1770's K-weighting and gating: a song reports after 45 s, or at its end if it played 10 s.
  - On a first play with normalization on, the desktop levels live from the running measurement (after 3 s, gliding over 1.5 s).
  - Normalization boosts at most 10 dB and cuts at most 20 dB; measurements outside −50…+5 LUFS (silence, broken streams) are dropped.
  - YouTube's embed can't be measured or boosted (YouTube normalizes its own videos), only turned down.
- **Trims:**
  - A song started from its top starts at its start trim; a resumed one starts where it was, but never before the trim.
  - The engine ends the song at its end trim (a timer while it plays, checked again on progress) and reports `Ended`, so the queue moves on as at a real end.
  - The pause that stops it isn't reported, or the player would show "paused" at the end of the queue.
- **Edits apply live:** saving the playing song's edit sends `SetTrackGain`/`SetEnd` (measured on Windows: −12 dB took the same stretch from −19.8 to −31.8 dB RMS).
- **Where it lives in the UI:**
  - Settings → Sound (a new category).
  - A sliders button beside the speed button in the player bar and the full player: the equalizer, "This song's sound and lyrics", and the rest of the settings.
  - "Edit sound and lyrics" in every song menu.
- **Lyrics (part 2):**
  - The offset moves every line, as Nuclear's does, but it is kept per song (Nuclear forgets it with each song).
  - Positive means lines show later. The lyrics view and seeking both honour it.
  - "Now" beside a line, while the song plays, sets the offset so that line shows at once. This is cloudstream's subtitle sync, applied to lyrics.
  - Editing lines keeps the times as typed until saved, so a half-typed time isn't lost; lines are sorted by time on save.
  - Stamping gives the next line the playing position, less the offset, so it shows exactly when stamped.
  - Plain lyrics can be split into lines to time them, and lyrics no source has can be typed in; the source's lyrics come back with "Use the source's lyrics again".
  - The editor is the song editor's Lyrics tab, reached from song menus, the player's sliders button, and "Fix the lyrics"/"Add lyrics" under the lyrics.
- **Subtitles (part 2):**
  - The delay both players had stays as the shift.
  - The desktop player lists the lines around the one playing. "Heard now" sets the delay from that line (cloudstream's sync), and a line's times and words can be changed or restored.
  - Edits are kept in the title's remembered state (`subtitleEdits`, by episode progress key, track label and language), so they come back when the episode is opened again.
  - Lines inside the video stream (embedded tracks) can only be shifted. Android's player renders tracks through ExoPlayer and keeps the delay only.
- **Reverb on Android** is the platform's `PresetReverb` on the output mix, sent from each player as an aux effect (`MODIFY_AUDIO_SETTINGS`), as RiPlay does. On desktop it is a ConvolverNode on a generated impulse (decaying noise, darker for rooms, brighter for the plate).
