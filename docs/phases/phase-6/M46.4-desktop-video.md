# M46.4 — Desktop video, trailers, the player bar and Search

**Asked by the user (2026-11-01):**
- After the first Windows build, they answered "Yes, video first" to "Build desktop video playback next?".
- While testing it, they added: "the player needs more outer controls like spotify, and trailers play like music video small embeds, see if you can do something about those".
- Then, about Search: "in search the source is a dropdown, it should be bubble tags you can select multiple", and "in search when blank then show the popular listings or something, dont leave it empty".

**Relevant docs:**
- M38, M44 and M45: the Android player, sessions and controls, whose protocol the desktop player speaks.
- M08: the YouTube embed host page.
- M41: Stremio streams and subtitles.
- M43: downloads played through `/local/`.
- ARCHITECTURE §6.2: the stream proxy.

## Goal
- **Watch works in the Windows build:**
  - Stremio streams, video-source links and finished downloads play in the app;
  - sessions behave as on Android: next/previous, episodes, sources, falling back to the next link, autoplay, resume, Continue watching;
  - the player has the controls and keyboard shortcuts a desktop player is expected to have.
- **Trailers play big:** a trailer (or any YouTube stream from an addon) opens in its own full-window player with YouTube's controls. It no longer replaces the music queue as a small "music video".
- **The player bar gets Spotify's controls:**
  - on wide windows: shuffle, previous, play, next and repeat, a seek bar with times, the favourite button, lyrics, a queue panel, volume, and opening the full player;
  - on phones: the bar gains the favourite and next buttons.
- **Search:**
  - sources are chips, any number chosen (none is every source);
  - before anything is typed, Search shows popular titles rather than a hint.

## Design

### The desktop player (`apps/raygun/src/video/`)
The player lives in the webview, as the music engines do on desktop.
- **Rust side** (`video.rs`, with `video_desktop.rs` for everything that isn't Android):
  - **Opening and updates:** `open_player` and `update_player` emit `VideoPlayerEvent { kind: open | update, json }`. The JSON is the same as the Android player gets: the request (`url`, `format`, `subtitles`, `startMs`, `titleState`, `chapters`, `prefs` …), and the updates `load`, `loading`, `episodes`, `sources`, `chapters` and `error`.
  - **The stream address:** before a payload is sent, the remote stream is registered with the proxy's new `/media/` route, which carries its headers. The webview can't set Referer or User-Agent, and hls.js needs CORS. Subtitles already go through `/subtitle/`, and downloads through `/local/`.
  - **Reports:** the player reports with the `video_player_report` command, in the exact JSON the Android player sends (`progress`, `ended`, `error`, `switched`, `closed`, and `request` with an action). Reports go into the same ordered queue as the JNI reports, so progress, Continue watching, the library mirror, sessions and title memory work unchanged.
- **The proxy's `/media/{token}/{scheme}/{host}/{path}` route** (`raygun-net`):
  - The path mirrors the upstream address, so relative addresses in playlists and manifests resolve to the proxy by themselves.
  - Every request under a token is fetched with that token's headers, with Range passed on.
  - A response that is an HLS playlist or a DASH manifest (found by sniffing the first bytes, whatever the type or extension) is rewritten: absolute and root-relative addresses (HLS `URI` lines and `URI="…"` attributes; DASH `BaseURL`, `media`, `initialization` and `sourceURL`) are pointed back into the route.
  - `OPTIONS` answers CORS preflights.
- **Engines:**
  - plain files go straight into `<video>`;
  - HLS uses hls.js and DASH uses dash.js, each loaded only when needed;
  - a file that the browser refuses and that has no format given is tried once with hls.js.
  - The `<video>` uses `crossOrigin="anonymous"`, so frames can be captured and the audio can be boosted.
- **The screen:**
  - **Layout:** a full-window overlay. The top bar has back, the title and the episode; Episodes and Sources open a side panel.
  - **Centre:** a spinner with the loading text, and a notice for errors with Retry, Sources and Play in another app.
  - **Bottom:** a time bar with what's buffered, chapter marks, the time under the pointer and dragging. The row below has play, previous/next episode, seek ± the seek step, volume (mute and slider, past 100 % with the boost), the time (a click shows the remaining time), the current chapter, then Speed, Subtitles, Audio, Quality, Aspect, Picture-in-picture and Full screen.
  - **Skip and next:** "Skip opening/ending/recap" appears over an opening, ending or recap chapter, or during the intro length, and auto-skip is honoured. The next-episode prompt counts down before the end; autoplay moves on at the end.
  - **Subtitles:** drawn by the app (`video/subtitles.ts` parses SRT, WebVTT and ASS/SSA), styled from `SubtitleStyle`, with a delay control. Embedded HLS/DASH subtitle tracks are read through hidden text tracks. A file can be added from the computer.
  - **Tracks picked by themselves:** subtitles by the subtitle language, the fallback and prefer/skip words; audio by the audio language and fallback. Each title remembers its speed, aspect, subtitle, audio and delay with `state` requests, in the Android player's format (`{language,label}` or `{off:true}`).
  - **Keys:**
    - Space/K play and pause;
    - ←/→ seek by the seek step, J/L by 10 s;
    - ↑/↓ volume, M mute;
    - F full screen, Esc leaves full screen, then the player;
    - Shift+N / Shift+P next and previous episode;
    - C turns subtitles on and off;
    - [ and ] change the speed;
    - , and . step a frame while paused;
    - 0–9 jump to that tenth;
    - S skips the chapter.
  - **Mouse:**
    - a click plays or pauses, a double click toggles full screen;
    - the wheel changes the volume;
    - the controls and pointer hide after `controlsSeconds`.
  - **Full screen** is the window's (Tauri `setFullscreen`).
- **Another app:** `video_player_external` launches mpv or VLC when installed (found on PATH or in their usual install folders), with the proxied address, the title, the start time and the first subtitle file. Arguments are passed without a shell. "Always use another app" opens it the same way. No progress comes back from those apps.

### Trailers
- **Opening:** `StremioService::play` with a YouTube stream no longer queues it in the music player. It pauses music and emits `YouTubeVideoEvent { videoId, title, subtitle, embedUrl }`.
- **The trailer player:** the UI opens `video/YouTubeOverlay.tsx`, a full-window player with its own iframe on the embed host page. A new `?controls=1` parameter turns on YouTube's controls and full-screen button.
- **Leaving:** Esc, back and the close button end it. The music queue is left as it was.
- **Elsewhere:** YouTube music videos in the queue still play in the dock, but the player sheet shows them at up to 4xl width on wide windows.

### The player bar (`player/MiniPlayer.tsx`)
- **Narrow windows:** artwork and title open the player; then the favourite button, play/pause and next. The progress line is kept.
- **768 px and wider, in Spotify's three zones:**
  - **Left:** artwork, title and artist, the favourite button.
  - **Centre:** shuffle, previous, a round play/pause, next and repeat; below them the seek bar with the elapsed and total time.
  - **Right:** lyrics (opens the player on its lyrics), the queue panel, a mute button with a volume slider, and the full player.
- **The queue panel** (`player/QueuePanel.tsx`) sits at the right above the bar: Now playing, then Next up. A click plays an item; each row has Remove; Clear removes all.
- `playerUiStore` gains whether the sheet opens on lyrics, the queue panel, and the volume to return to when unmuting.

### Search
- **Source chips** (`search/CatalogSearch.tsx`):
  - "All sources", then one chip per source, folded after 12 (chosen ones always show).
  - The choice is remembered per medium on the device.
  - Several sources search together (`catalog_global_search` takes `sources`); exactly one searches with its filters and tags, as the dropdown's one source did.
  - "More" on a source's row chooses that source alone.
- **Popular before typing:**
  - A blank `global_search` gives each source's first popular page.
  - A blank `catalog_search` without filters gives the source's popular titles, page by page.
  - Search → All shows the addons' catalogs, top podcasts and the sources' popular rows. Music and the library still ask for a query (there's nothing popular to list for them).

- **Progress from mpv/VLC.**
- **Websites, TTS, the folder picker and share/copy on desktop:** next desktop milestones.
- **Brightness and the Android gestures,** which have no desktop equivalent.

## Decisions
- **The player lives in the webview, not libmpv:**
  - **What it covers:** H.264/VP9/AV1 with AAC/Opus in MP4/WebM, plus HLS and DASH through hls.js and dash.js. Those are almost all addon and source streams.
  - **The rest:** mpv or VLC, which the player offers when a format fails.
  - **Why not libmpv:** it would mean shipping a native player and drawing it under the webview, a milestone of its own.
- **One protocol for both players:**
  - Rust builds the same requests and updates for the desktop as for Android; only the transport differs (events instead of an intent and JNI).
  - Reports go into the same ordered queue.
  - So sessions, fallback, resume, Continue watching, the library mirror, AniSkip and title memory needed no desktop branches.
- **The `/media/` route mirrors the upstream path:**
  - Relative addresses in HLS and DASH resolve into the proxy by themselves.
  - HLS addresses are rewritten anyway, so they are right after redirects; DASH ones only when absolute or root-relative, since relative ones are templates resolved against nested `BaseURL`s.
  - **Sniffing:** playlists are found from their first bytes (`#EXTM3U`, `<MPD`), because hosts serve them as `text/plain` or under disguised names.
  - **Limits:** tokens are random and only resolve to registered headers, the route only fetches web addresses, and the listener is loopback.
- **Subtitles are drawn by the app:**
  - `<track>` only takes WebVTT and can't be styled from Settings or delayed.
  - SRT, VTT and ASS are parsed into cues and drawn over the picture with the subtitle style. HLS/DASH subtitle tracks go through hidden text tracks.
- **Trailers no longer touch the music queue:** they replaced the queue with a one-item "music video" before. Now they open their own player, with YouTube's controls on the M08 embed host page (`?controls=1`), and music pauses.
- **The bar picks its layout by width,** not by platform. A phone in landscape or a tablet at 768 px+ gets the full bar; a narrow desktop window gets the phone bar.
- **Several sources search without filters:** filters belong to one source (each has its own), so they only show when one source is chosen.
