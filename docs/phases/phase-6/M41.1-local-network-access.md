# M41.1 — Local network access on Android 17

**Relevant docs:** M41 (Stremio addons; its Decisions recorded the limitation this fixes), M20 (LAN remote), [Local network permission](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/local-network-permission) and [what counts as the local network](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/local-network-definition) on developer.android.com.

## Goal
- **Addons on the user's network work:** a self-hosted Stremio addon (`http://192.168.…`, `http://nas.local`) can be added and used on Android 17.
- **The LAN remote works on Android 17:** other devices on the Wi-Fi can reach it.
- **No silent time-outs:** without the permission, Raygun says what to allow instead of failing after 20 s with "operation timed out".

## Background
- **Android 17 local network protection:** apps targeting SDK 37 need the runtime permission `android.permission.ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK` (`Manifest.permission.ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK`, added in API 37, protection level `dangerous`, in the `NEARBY_DEVICES` group).
  - **What it covers:** outgoing and incoming TCP, and UDP unicast, multicast and broadcast, with any host on the local network.
  - **What counts as local:** RFC 1918, link-local, CGNAT (100.64/10), multicast and broadcast. Loopback and the internet don't count.
  - **Without it:** TCP connections time out.
- **Android's own dialog** reads "Allow Raygun to find, connect to, and determine the relative position of nearby devices?". In the app's settings it is Permissions → Nearby devices.
- **Measured on the Pixel_4 emulator (Android 17) before this change:**
  - the app timed out on the PC at 10.0.2.2, while the shell user reached it;
  - with the remote on, a connection through the emulator's router (`adb emu redir`, arriving from 10.0.2.2) timed out after 12 s, while `adb forward` (loopback) answered in 46 ms;
  - so the remote needed the permission as well.

## Design
- **`raygun-net::local_network`** (pure, tested): which hosts are local.
  - `is_local_ip`: Android's ranges. RFC 1918, 169.254/16, 100.64/10, multicast, 255.255.255.255, IPv6 unique-local (fc00::/7), link-local and multicast, and IPv4-mapped forms of these.
  - `host_scope`: an address or an mDNS `.local` name is `Local`; loopback, `localhost` and public addresses are `Elsewhere`; other names need DNS.
  - `reaches_local_network(url)`: looks names up in DNS with a 3 s limit. Android answers DNS without the permission. A failed lookup counts as not local.
- **`raygun-audio` plugin** (Kotlin, on the existing Tauri permission machinery):
  - the `localNetwork` alias;
  - `localNetworkState`, which reports whether the platform enforces the permission (Android 17+ and target SDK 37+), and whether it is granted or blocked;
  - `requestLocalNetwork`, which shows the system dialog;
  - `openAppSettings`, which opens `ACTION_APPLICATION_DETAILS_SETTINGS`.
  - Rust exposes these on `NativeAudio`.
- **`raygun-app::local_network::LocalNetwork`** (managed state):
  - `access(address)` (`needed`, `granted`, `blocked`) and `request()`;
  - `check(url, message)` fails at once with the message when the URL is local and the app may not reach it;
  - `allowed()`.
  - A grant is remembered for the process; a denial is asked again each time. Other platforms report "not enforced, granted".
- **Where it applies:**
  - `StremioService::get_json`, so adding and every request to an addon, checks with "Allow local network access to reach addons on your network".
  - Playing a direct stream checks with "Allow local network access to play videos from your network".
  - `RemoteService::set_enabled(true)` refuses with "Allow local network access so devices on your network can reach the remote".
  - `status()` reports the same message while the remote is on without access, for example after the permission was taken back.
- **Commands:** `local_network_access(address?)`, `local_network_request()`, `local_network_open_settings()`.
- **UI** (`src/network/`):
  - `useLocalNetworkGate(reason)` guards an action: if access is needed and not granted, `LocalNetworkSheet` explains why in Raygun's words, then Continue shows Android's dialog;
  - on a grant the action runs;
  - on a denial the sheet says what to allow;
  - when Android says it won't ask again, the sheet offers "Open settings" with the path.
  - Used by the Addons page's add-by-address form and by the remote switch when turning it on.
- **Manifest:** `<uses-permission android:name="android.permission.ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK" />` in the app's `AndroidManifest.xml`.

## Decisions
- **Ask only when it's needed.**
  - Addons: when the typed address is on the local network.
  - Remote: when it's turned on.
  - Raygun never asks at launch, and never for public addons. Suggested addons (Cinemeta, OpenSubtitles) skip the check.
- **Raygun explains before Android asks.** The system dialog talks about finding "nearby devices" and their "relative position", which says nothing about addons or a remote. Android's docs also give apps that present a rationale a reset of the denial count.
- **Android is always asked first.** The sheet offers Continue even when Android last said it won't ask again. Tauri caches that answer, and it goes stale: the user may have reset the app's permissions, or Android may auto-reset an unused app. "Open settings" appears only when this request itself comes back blocked. The cost is one tap in the truly blocked case.
- **Fail fast in Rust as well as in the UI.**
  - An addon added before the update, or an addon whose permission was taken back later, is still reached on every catalog, meta and stream request. `check` turns the 20 s time-out into an immediate, clear row error.
  - Once access is granted the check is one atomic load: Android restarts the app when a permission is revoked, so a grant can be remembered for the process.
  - Before a grant, each addon request to a host name costs one DNS lookup, which the OS cache answers.
- **The remote isn't asked about at startup.** `RemoteService::start` runs on the main thread during setup, and the plugin call needs that same thread. `status()` reports the missing permission instead. Turning the switch off and on asks again.
- **DNS decides for names:**
  - `nas.lan`, a single-label name or a public name that resolves to a private address all count as local;
  - `.local` names count as local without a lookup, since resolving one needs the permission.
  - Known gap: a global IPv6 address on the same subnet ("directly connected routes") is local to Android but looks public here. Such a request still times out without the permission.
- **The permission is declared in the app's manifest**, next to `INTERNET` and the multicast lock, rather than in the plugin's.
- **Out of scope, follow-ups:**
  - device sync (M21: mDNS and Noise over the LAN) needs the same gate, but it is on hold and off by default;
  - extensions can't reach private hosts anyway (`is_public_host`);
  - the Watch home caches its rows for 10 minutes, so after allowing access in system settings, a LAN addon's row shows its old error until it refreshes or the app restarts.

## Verification
- **Checks:**
  - `cargo test -p raygun-app -p raygun-net` passes, including `bindings_are_up_to_date`;
  - clippy reports nothing in the changed files;
  - rustfmt, eslint, prettier and `tsc` are clean on the changed files;
  - the Vitest suites `network`, `RemoteCard` and `Stremio` pass.
  - The workspace-wide `cargo clippy -D warnings` was blocked by unrelated in-progress M42 code (`raygun-ext-host/src/video.rs`) at the time.
- **New tests:**
  - `raygun-net::local_network` (local and non-local addresses, the edges of each range, host scope by address and name, URLs);
  - `raygun-app::local_network` (typed addresses as URLs, when access is needed);
  - six Vitest cases in `network/LocalNetwork.test.tsx`: adding a LAN addon once allowed, the denied message with nothing added, the blocked path to settings, public addons not asking, the remote switch's denial then grant, and turning the remote off without asking.
- 📱 **Pixel_4 emulator (Android 17), debug build, PC test addon at `http://10.0.2.2:7011`** (Python, bound to the PC's 127.0.0.1):
  - **After install:** `ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK` is requested and not granted. The remote, left on from before, shows "Allow local network access so devices on your network can reach the remote".
  - **Adding the LAN addon:**
    - Raygun's sheet explains;
    - Continue shows Android's "nearby devices" dialog;
    - "Don't allow" leaves the sheet saying "Allow local network access to reach addons on your network." and adds nothing;
    - Continue → Allow adds "LAN Test Addon", and its "LAN Test · Movies" row appears on the Watch home with "Big Buck Bunny (LAN)".
  - **Remote:**
    - with access, turning it on asks nothing;
    - a connection from 10.0.2.2 (`adb emu redir add tcp:8831:8820`) answers 200 in 16 ms, where it timed out before;
    - after a second denial, the sheet says Android won't ask again, and "Open settings" opens Raygun's App info;
    - Permissions → Nearby devices → Allow there, then the switch turns on without asking in the same process, and the LAN row loads after a relaunch.
  - **Android won't ask again** (second build, with the "always ask first" change; the permission was revoked and set `user-fixed`):
    - adding `http://192.168.1.250:7000` shows Raygun's sheet with Continue;
    - Continue shows no system dialog;
    - the sheet then says "Allow local network access to reach addons on your network." and "Android won't ask again…", and offers Open settings.
  - **Revoked (`pm revoke`, which restarts the app):** the LAN row on the Watch home reads "This catalog failed: Allow local network access to reach addons on your network" at once, not after 20 s.
  - **Clean-up:**
    - the LAN addon was removed and the remote turned off;
    - the permission was revoked and its flags cleared;
    - port forwards and redirects were removed, and the test server was stopped.
