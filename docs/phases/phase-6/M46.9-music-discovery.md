# M46.9 — Music discovery and tags

**Asked by the user (2026-11-02):**
- Popular and trending music on Home and Browse, and songs tagged by genre and mood "like spotify" (Rock, Rap, Jazz, Breakcore, Energetic, Chill…), from open sources.
- Added while it was built: "at the top of the app have this thing that spotify has, file menu and back and forward and home and search and a browse button", with screenshots of Spotify's top bar (⋯ → File, Edit, View, Playback, Help) and its Browse all tiles.

**Relevant docs:** M14 (streams for tracks found elsewhere), M18 (favorites, playlists), M42.1 (Browse), M46.5 (layout lists), M46.8 (song tables, artist pages).

## Goal
- **Charts:** popular and trending songs from open charts, playable like any search result (a stream is found when one plays):
  - Apple Music's most played, per country;
  - Deezer's top tracks, overall and per genre;
  - ListenBrainz's most listened this week and month, worldwide.
- **Home:** "Popular now" and "Trending" rows, as sections that can be moved or hidden (`homeSections`).
- **Browse → Music** (a new Browse tab):
  - a row per chart;
  - a "Genres & moods" grid of coloured tiles (Spotify's Browse all);
  - a tile opens the tag's page: that genre's charts where a source has one, and your songs with the tag.
- **Tags on songs:**
  - genres come from open sources (MusicBrainz recording and artist genres, Deezer album genres);
  - moods are derived from genres (Chill, Energetic, Happy, Sad, Dark, Romantic, Party, Focus, Workout, Sleep);
  - your saved songs (favourites, playlists, library) are tagged in the background, gently;
  - song tables get a row of tag chips that filters the list, and a Tags column;
  - a song's details list its tags.
- **Settings → Browse → Music:** the charts' country (automatic from the app language, or picked) and background tagging on or off.
- **The desktop top bar (Spotify's):**
  - Raygun draws the window's top edge:
    - the ⋯ menu (File, Edit, View, Playback, Help, each with its items beside it);
    - Back and Forward;
    - Home;
    - the search field ("What do you want to play?") with a Browse button at its end that opens Browse all;
    - Updates and Downloads;
    - the window's own buttons.
  - Empty parts move the window, and a double click maximizes it.
  - Settings → Appearance → Window chooses Raygun's bar or the system's title bar, and the zoom (Ctrl+=, Ctrl+-, Ctrl+0).

## Design
- **Extension kinds (SDK and host):**
  - `music.charts`:
    - `charts({ region })` gives `{ charts: [{ id, name, description?, artworkUrl?, tag? }] }`; `tag` names the genre a chart belongs to;
    - `chart({ id, region, limit })` gives `{ tracks: Track[] }`.
  - `music.tags`: `tags(query)` gives `{ tags: [{ name, weight? }] }` for a track (title, artist, album, duration).
  - The host calls them like the other music kinds and skips disabled sources.
- **First-party extensions:**
  - `extensions/deezer`: charts (top tracks overall and per genre) and tags (the album's genres, found by a track search);
  - `extensions/apple-charts`: most played songs per country, from Apple's public RSS feed;
  - `extensions/listenbrainz`: sitewide most listened recordings (week, month), artwork from the Cover Art Archive;
  - `extensions/musicbrainz` 1.x gains `music.tags`: the recording's genres, or the artist's when the recording has none, one request a second.
- **App (`raygun-app/src/discovery.rs`, `DiscoveryService`):**
  - Charts: every provider's list at once (a few at a time), cached for an hour; a chart's tracks are cached for half an hour.
  - Tags:
    - Asked for each provider, merged by name with genre spelling normalized (`hip hop`, `Hip-Hop` → `Hip-Hop`). Moods are added from a genre → mood table (`moods.rs`).
    - Stored per track key in `raygun-db::track_tags`, a device-local table, not synced: they're derived data.
    - A background tagger runs through saved songs without tags, one at a time, resting between songs, and starts again when songs are saved.
  - Commands:
    - `music_charts(region)`, `music_chart(provider, id, region)`;
    - `music_track_tags(track)`, `music_tags_for(keys)` (stored tags for many songs);
    - `music_tag_summary()` (your tags with counts), `music_tag_tracks(tag)`.
  - `Settings::discovery` (`DiscoveryPrefs`): `region` (empty = automatic), `tag_songs`.
- **UI:**
  - `music/ChartRow.tsx`: a horizontal row of songs (play, Plus, right-click menu).
  - `pages/BrowseMusic.tsx`: the Music tab.
  - `pages/MusicTagPage.tsx`: `/music/tag?name=`.
  - `music/TagChips.tsx`: the chips on song tables, which filter in the UI using the stored tags.
  - Home sections `popularMusic` and `trendingMusic`.
  - The Browse tab id is `music`; `LAYOUT_BROWSE_TABS` gains it, after Search.

## Decisions
- **Charts:**
  - Three first-party sources, all keyless:
    - Apple's most played per country;
    - Deezer's top and per-genre charts (Deezer picks the country from the request);
    - ListenBrainz's sitewide week, month and year.
  - Apple's per-genre feed is the old iTunes sales chart, so only "most played" is used.
  - Apple's feed answered when Raygun identified itself (as MusicBrainz and LRCLIB already do), so the extension sends Raygun's own User-Agent.
- **Tags:**
  - Sources tag in their own words, so `genres.rs` keeps one vocabulary (78 genres, 10 moods) with the spellings each source uses. Tags outside it ("seen live", "british") are dropped.
  - Combined genres ("Techno/House") count as each part.
  - Moods come from sources that name them (MusicBrainz's "happy") and from the two strongest genres, at half weight.
  - A song keeps at most four genres and three moods.
  - Tags are stored per resolver track key in `local_track_tags`, device-local: they're derived and cheap to find again.
  - An empty list is stored when sources answered but found nothing. When none could answer, the song is skipped this session and tried again later.
  - The tagger walks favorites, then playlist songs, then the library, resting 3 s between songs. After five failures in a row it pauses for ten minutes. Saving songs or turning tagging on wakes it.
  - A library file's own genre counts as one source.
- **Browse all:**
  - It's Browse's first tab.
  - Kind tiles open Search with nothing typed, which already shows each kind's popular titles; Music opens a page of every chart.
  - Genres shown before "Show all": moods, a featured list, every genre a chart covers, and the user's own tags.
  - Tile colours come from the name, so a genre always has its colour.
- **The top bar:**
  - The window is frameless (`decorations: false`), and Tauri resizes frameless windows on Windows itself.
  - "The system's title bar" turns decorations back on at start.
  - The bar uses `data-tauri-drag-region="deep"`, so its buttons stay clickable.
  - On desktop it holds the search field, and Browse's own field hides.
  - Zoom keys are left to the readers, which zoom their pages with them.
  - Forward is known from the router's history index: a new page drops the entries ahead.
  - Edit works on the last focused field. Paste goes through the clipboard API, as the right-click menu does.

- **Also in this milestone** (asked on 2026-11-02 while it was built):
  - Song tables' column dividers sit on the left edge of the column they resize, follow the pointer while dragged (the grid changes live), and move with the arrow keys. Before, only the cell resized, and the column grew away from the divider.
  - Source rows' loading placeholders use the cover grid's size.
  - On desktop the rail and the Now playing panel stay at every window size: the rail shows covers only below 1024 px, and the panel narrows to at most 30 % of the window.
  - On desktop, Browse's tabs share a row with its ⋯ menu.
- **Chart genres:** a genre outside the vocabulary keeps the source's name, so its chart never shows among the main charts. "Asian Music" did, so Asian joined the vocabulary.
