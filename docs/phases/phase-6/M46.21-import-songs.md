# M46.21 — Importing songs: Spotify links, lists and screenshots

**Asked by the user (2026-11-02):** "add a way to import spotify playlists and songs into raygun, maybe playlists links, and then it imports the song names and authors and finds matching sources per each song, and option to read songs from screenshots as a feature".

## Goal
- **Library → Audio → Playlists → Import songs** opens a sheet with three ways in:
  - **Link:** a Spotify playlist, album or song link (`open.spotify.com/…`, with or without `intl-xx/` or `embed/`, or `spotify:…`). Read from Spotify's public embed page: title, artists and length of each song. Playlists stop at their first 100 songs, and the sheet says so.
  - **Text or CSV:** pasted lines ("Artist - Title", "Title by Artist", numbers and lengths ignored), or a CSV such as Exportify's (`Track Name`, `Artist Name(s)`, `Album Name`, `Duration (ms)`) or any with title and artist columns.
  - **Screenshots:** pictures of a song list from Spotify, YouTube Music, Apple Music and the like. Text lines are read, then grouped into rows:
    - the list's column is the left edge most lines share;
    - a new row starts after a gap of 2.2 line heights;
    - the first line is the title, the next the artist, with "E" badges and "• album" dropped.
- **Review:** each song is matched against the music sources (`music.metadata` search, scored by the resolver's matcher).
  - A match fills in the song's cover, album and length, which helps playback find the right recording; a source's direct stream is kept.
  - The list fills in as matching goes, a mark beside each song (found on <source>, or not found yet). Songs can be unticked.
  - The name is editable. "Make a playlist" saves it and opens it. Unmatched songs are kept: the resolver looks for them when they play.

## Reading pictures
- **Windows:** the system's own engine, `Windows.Media.Ocr`, in the user's languages. No download.
- **Android and others:** ocrs (MIT/Apache-2.0), a Rust OCR engine.
  - Its two ONNX models (2.5 MB and 9.7 MB) are in the model catalogue as `ocrs-latin`, pinned by SHA-256. They download the first time ("Download the text reader") and show in Settings → AI.
  - It reads Latin script.

## Decisions
- **Spotify:** only its public embed page is read: no account, no API keys, no private endpoints. Private playlists can't be read, and the sheet says so.
- **Matching:**
  - 8 songs per call, 4 at a time inside it, so a long list shows progress and a slow source can't hold it up (searches give up after 12 s, M46.17).
  - A YouTube embed match isn't kept as the song's source (it's a video); the resolver picks a stream when the song plays.
- **Screenshots:**
  - OCR lines from a screenshot are deduplicated across pictures, so overlapping screenshots of one long list don't repeat songs.
  - Pictures are prepared before reading: grey, dark themes inverted (both engines read dark-on-light best; Windows' engine found nothing in a dark screenshot until it was inverted), and pictures narrower than 1600 px doubled (small text reads far better larger).
- **Dependencies:** `image` is now built for Android too (ocrs needs it); the `windows` crate gains the OCR features.

## Verification
- **Tests:**
  - parsing: Spotify links in their forms, embed pages (playlist, single track, a missing page), Exportify and simple CSVs, pasted lines, screenshot rows (from Windows OCR output of Spotify's embed);
  - UI: a link read, matched (one found, one not), unticked, renamed and saved; a failed link as a calm issue, then pasted lines; downloading the text reader first where it isn't built in.
  - All app tests (427), clippy, `cargo test --workspace`. The ocrs path type-checks for `x86_64-linux-android`.
- **Live checks:**
  - `spotify_live`: Today's Top Hits read 50 songs (Loser, Tame Impala…); Thriller (album) 9.
  - `screenshot_live` on a real Spotify screenshot (420 px wide, dark): 14 songs with their artists ("Loser" — Tame Impala, "Housewife" — Tate McRae, "expectations (feat. Chappell Roan)" — Olivia Rodrigo, Chappell Roan…). Without the preparation the same picture gave 40 noisier lines at half size, and a dark one gave none.
- **Windows (release build):**
  - Library → Audio → Playlists showed "Import songs" under "New playlist".
  - The Thriller album link was read and all 9 songs were found in the sources, each marked.
  - "Make a playlist of 9 songs" opened the playlist with covers, album and lengths filled in, and it played (Wanna Be Startin' Somethin', 6:03). The test playlist was deleted afterwards.
  - Choosing screenshots opened the system picture picker. A blurry, downscaled JPEG of the app's own list read only one song, which led to the preparation above. With the preparation, the same Spotify screenshot read 14 songs in the app and 13 were found in the sources (cancelled; nothing saved).
- **Android (debug build):** Screenshots downloaded the text reader on first use (ocrs, 12.2 MB, its SHA-256 checked). It read 44 lines of a Spotify screenshot into 13 songs and found all 13; cancelled, and the test picture deleted.
